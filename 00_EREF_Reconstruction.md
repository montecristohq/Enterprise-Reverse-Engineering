# EREF — RECONSTRUCTION STAGE

**This is a synthesis prompt, not a research prompt.**

Every other EREF prompt commissions research. This one does not. It operates on volumes that already exist, and it is given to the synthesiser with those volumes in hand — not to a research agent.

That distinction is why this file carries `00` rather than a volume number, and why its opening apparatus differs from every other prompt in the framework. **Do not perform new research.** If a material gap appears, record it as a known unknown; do not go and fill it, because a finding sourced here has not been through the evidence discipline the volumes applied.

**Gap check.** If a volume in the declared configuration is missing, **stop and say so.** Synthesis across a hole produces a document that looks complete and is not, and the hole will be invisible to every subsequent reader.

---

## §B — INHERITED EVIDENCE (binding)

**Evidence labels applied in the volumes carry through unchanged.**

The synthesis stage does not re-grade. It does not promote a HYPOTHESIS to a CONFIRMED FACT because the assembled picture now looks coherent — coherence is not evidence, and a reconstruction is exactly where that temptation is strongest. Where the volumes labelled something UNKNOWN, it remains UNKNOWN here and appears in the known-unknowns register.

**The governing metric is inherited, not re-chosen.** Every figure in this stage is expressed on the basis the volumes used.

**Where the objective is playbook extraction,** ADOPT / ADAPT / REJECT verdicts are **consolidated, not generated.** A verdict that appears for the first time in reconstruction has not been reasoned from evidence; it has been reasoned from summary. If a mechanism was not adjudicated in its volume, record it as unadjudicated rather than adjudicating it now.

---

## §C — RECONCILIATION (perform before writing)

The volumes were researched independently and will disagree. Work through every conflict and classify it:

**Genuine error** — one volume is wrong. Establish which governs and why. **Correct it at source with a visible inline note**, then record it here. A reader meets the error in the body of the study, so the correction belongs there and not only in an appendix.

**Dual-value figure** — both values are defensible: an announced price against a settled one, a headline figure against a filing figure, a company statement against an audited one. **State one canonical value, explain the other, and say which to use for what.**

**Vintage difference** — not a conflict at all. A figure moved because the underlying quantity moved. **Present as a dated series** so a reader does not mistake movement for disagreement.

**Known unknowns** — carry forward every UNKNOWN from every volume into one register. On a long study this register is not an admission of weakness; it is the document's warrant.

## §D — THE RECONSTRUCTION

**Use the completed volumes. Do not merely summarise them. Reconstruct the enterprise as one coherent system.**

The test for every section below: *does this say something no single volume could say?* If it restates a volume, cut it.

### 1. Enterprise in one sentence

### 2. Enterprise in one paragraph

### 3. Enterprise in one page

### 4. Complete system map
Environment / market → institutional structure → capital, people, technology, assets → products and services → distribution → customers → value delivery → revenue → costs → profit and cash → reinvestment → competitive advantage → growth, scale and strategic evolution.

### 5. Institutional kernel
The minimum legal, governance and regulatory structure necessary for the enterprise to exist at all.

### 6. Commercial kernel
The customer, problem, acquisition channel, pricing model and retention mechanism that matter most.

### 7. Product kernel
The core product or service, and the value-delivery chain.

### 8. Operating kernel
The minimum processes and assets required to deliver the value proposition reliably.

### 9. Technology kernel
The critical technology and data systems — **and the ranked TREF candidate list**, carried from the technology volume.

### 10. Economic kernel
The business reduced to its core economic equation and driver tree.

### 11. Management kernel
Who makes the most consequential decisions, and how incentives shape behaviour.

### 12. Moat kernel
Rank the true sources of durable advantage and show how they reinforce one another. **Apply discriminating scepticism**: where the evidence supports a genuine advantage, rank it accordingly; where a claimed advantage did not survive testing in its volume, say so plainly. A ranking in which everything scores alike has said nothing.

### 13. Bottleneck
The single most important current constraint, and the likely next one.

### 14. Dependency model
Critical external dependencies ranked by replaceability and failure impact.

### 15. Risk kernel
The few risks capable of structural impairment — not the register, which lives in the strategic volume.

### 16. Flywheel model
Positive and negative feedback loops, with the evidence for each.

### 17. Reconstruction test
If the enterprise disappeared tomorrow, what must be rebuilt? For each major component estimate time, capital, talent, regulatory complexity, technical complexity, operational complexity, and relationship or network difficulty.

### 18. Minimum viable enterprise
The smallest organisation that could reproduce the core customer value.

### 19. Full-scale reconstruction
What additional systems create the present enterprise's scale, resilience and moat.

### 20. Essential versus incidental
Classify major features as essential; useful but replaceable; historical accident or path dependence; unnecessary complexity; or unknown.

### 21. What the market misunderstands
Five to ten misconceptions. For each: the conventional view, the underlying reality, the evidence, why the misconception persists, and the implication.

### 22. Hidden strategic asset
The most valuable component conventional analysis underweights.

### 23. Natural experiments
Where did the enterprise **run the experiment itself** — two comparable businesses with divergent outcomes, markets entered and exited, products built and killed? State what each demonstrates that no external comparison could. *Draw on Volumes IX and X where commissioned; on the strategic volume otherwise.*

### 24. Final scorecard
Score 1–10 and **explain every score**: institutional architecture · governance · market quality · customer value · distribution · pricing power · product · operations · technology · data · financial quality · unit economics · capital efficiency · management · culture · moat · resilience · strategic optionality · overall enterprise quality.

### 25. Final verdict
- What has actually been built?
- Why has it worked?
- What is hardest to reproduce? What is easiest?
- What is the real economic engine? What is the real moat?
- What is the biggest bottleneck? What is the largest structural risk?
- What is the most underappreciated asset?
- What will determine whether the enterprise compounds over the next decade?
- What should a founder or operator learn from its design?

## §E — WHERE THE OBJECTIVE IS PLAYBOOK EXTRACTION

Add, after §D:

**26. The consolidated verdict table.** Every ADOPT / ADAPT / REJECT from every volume, in one place, with the volume of origin. Group by verdict; index by mechanism.

**27. What transfers, and what does not.** The mechanisms worth the whole study, and the dependencies that kill the rest. For each REJECT, its constructive half: having established what cannot be done, establish what should be done instead.

**28. The sequenced build order.** Stages with **tests, not instructions** — what would tell the reader to proceed, and what would tell them to stop. Front-load the cheap irreversible decisions; defer everything purchasable.

**29. What this study cannot tell you.** The questions that remain open, and which of them are answerable only by evidence from the reader's own market rather than from the subject.

## Final standard

> **A sophisticated reader can mentally disassemble and reconstruct the enterprise.**

And, where the objective is extraction:

> **The reader knows which mechanisms to carry across, which to change, which to refuse — and which institution in their own market must play the role that one plays in the subject's.**
