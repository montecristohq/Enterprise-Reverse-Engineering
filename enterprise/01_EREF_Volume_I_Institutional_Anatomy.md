# EREF — Standalone Volume Research Prompt

## Research Target

- **Company:** [COMPANY NAME]
- **Industry / industries:** [INDUSTRY OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Primary geographies:** [GEOGRAPHIES OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Research date:** [CURRENT DATE]
- **Special focus:** [OPTIONAL]

Conduct an **institutional-grade enterprise reverse-engineering study**.

This is not a conventional company profile, SWOT analysis, short equity-research note, or generic strategy exercise.

The objective is to take the enterprise apart component by component until its underlying machinery is understood.

## Non-Negotiable Depth Rule

Depth follows complexity and importance, not an arbitrary page or token budget.

If a subject requires 3 pages, write 3 pages. If it requires 20 pages, write 20 pages. If it requires 50 pages, write 50 pages.

Do not shorten later sections because earlier sections became long. A section ends only when the subject is adequately understood.

> **Completeness before concision.**

If output limits prevent completion, stop at a logical boundary and continue later without compressing the remaining work.

## Evidence Labels

Use:

- **CONFIRMED FACT**
- **COMPANY CLAIM**
- **THIRD-PARTY ESTIMATE**
- **ANALYTICAL INFERENCE**
- **HYPOTHESIS**
- **UNKNOWN**

Never present inference as fact.

## Source Priority

Prioritize annual reports, regulatory/securities filings, investor presentations, earnings materials, official corporate documents, public contracts, official product/service documentation, regulators, registries, court records, patent/trademark databases, procurement records, official technical sources, partner/customer/supplier sources, academic/industry research, reputable journalism, and specialist publications.

Triangulate important findings.

## Universal Questions

For every material component ask:

- **What?**
- **How?**
- **Who?**
- **Where?**
- **When?**
- **Why?**

Then analyze:

- **Structure**
- **Flow**
- **Control**
- **Economics**
- **Risk**

## Mandatory Trace Rules

### Follow the money
Identify whose money it is, which entity controls it, where it resides, when ownership/economic entitlement changes, when revenue is recognized, what costs are deducted, and who bears risk.

### Follow the physical flow
Trace materials, inventory, production, warehousing, logistics, delivery, returns, and service where relevant.

### Follow the data
Trace generation, storage, processing, access, analytics, governance, privacy, and decision use.

### Follow the legal entity
Identify which entity owns assets, signs contracts, employs people, holds licenses, earns revenue, bears liabilities, owns IP, and pays taxes.

### Follow the authority
Identify who can make, approve, block, override, or terminate important decisions.

### Follow the revenue
Customer need → commercial event → pricing → gross transaction/billing → direct costs → recognized revenue → contribution → operating expenses → profit → cash.

## Industry Adaptation

Adapt the framework to the company. Do not force fintech, software, manufacturing, banking, or other sector concepts where they do not apply.

## Anti-Speculation Rule

If evidence is unavailable, state what is known, label inference, preserve uncertainty, and identify evidence needed to resolve it.

# Volume I — Institutional Anatomy
## Corporate, Ownership, Governance, Legal & Regulatory

**Execute Volume I only. Do not begin Volume II.**

Primary question:

> **What is the legal and institutional skeleton of the enterprise, and who ultimately controls it?**

## I.1 Corporate History
Reconstruct founding, original business model, financing, acquisitions, divestitures, restructurings, geographic expansion, listing, leadership changes, crises, regulatory milestones, strategic pivots, and current state.

For each major event identify structural change, new capability, new dependency, legal/regulatory consequence, and path dependence.

## I.2 Corporate Group Structure
Map parent, holding companies, operating subsidiaries, regional entities, licensed entities, IP entities, employment entities, manufacturing/distribution entities, financing entities, JVs, SPVs, and materially relevant dormant entities.

For each: legal name, jurisdiction, ownership, immediate parent, function, assets, liabilities, employees, revenue responsibility, tax residence, regulated status, permits/licenses, and major contracts where public.

## I.3 Ownership
Analyze founders, families, institutions, strategic holders, governments, employee ownership, public float, share classes, preferred rights, dilution, and ownership changes.

Explicitly separate **economic ownership** from **voting control**.

## I.4 Governance
Analyze board, chair, CEO, committees, independence, succession, tenure, executive committee, governance controversies, and board expertise.

## I.5 Decision Rights
Answer:
- Who can make what decisions?
- Who can stop what decisions?

Map capital allocation, M&A, divestitures, executive appointments, debt, dividends/buybacks, geographic expansion, product/facility investment, risk, and regulatory authority.

## I.6 Executive Compensation
Analyze salary, bonus, equity, long-term incentives, performance metrics, pensions/perquisites, founder incentives, alignment, and incentive distortions.

## I.7 Legal Architecture
Analyze customer contracts, supplier contracts, distribution agreements, IP ownership, patents, trademarks, licensing, outsourcing, labor arrangements, intercompany agreements, related parties, litigation, antitrust, product liability, and tax disputes.

## I.8 Regulatory Architecture
Where material, map regulators, licenses, permits, certifications, concessions, product approvals, capital requirements, reporting duties, safety/quality obligations, geographic scope, and renewal/termination risk.

## I.9 Own Capability vs Partner Dependency
For major regulated/institutional capabilities determine whether the company owns, licenses, outsources, partners, uses a JV, or relies on government-granted access.

## I.10 Regulation as Cost, Constraint or Moat
Assess barrier to entry, trust, scarcity, capital burden, compliance burden, protected position, and geographic advantage.

## I.11 Institutional Dependencies
Map governments, regulators, banks, lenders, insurers, critical suppliers, utilities, landlords, standards bodies, exchanges, strategic customers, infrastructure owners, and major vendors. Rank criticality and replaceability.

## I.12 Tax & Intercompany Architecture
Where evidence permits: tax residence, transfer pricing, IP royalties, management charges, cost allocations, withholding tax, incentives, effective tax rate, and uncertain positions.

## I.13 Institutional Risk
Analyze license loss, litigation, government intervention, tax disputes, IP risk, antitrust, partner termination, regulatory change, export controls, and parent/subsidiary contagion.

## Required Deliverables
1. Corporate evolution timeline
2. Full corporate tree
3. Simplified economic entity tree
4. Ownership/voting-control map
5. Governance map
6. Decision-rights matrix
7. Legal responsibility map
8. Regulatory/permit map
9. Own-vs-partner capability matrix
10. Institutional dependency map
11. Tax/intercompany map where possible
12. Institutional risk register
13. Key unknowns
14. Ten most important findings

## Final Questions
- Where does actual control sit?
- Which legal entity is strategically most important?
- Which permission or institutional relationship is hardest to reproduce?
- What is the most dangerous dependency?
- Which part of the institutional structure is most misunderstood?

**Completion test:** A reader should understand who owns, controls, regulates, legally constitutes, and institutionally enables the enterprise.
