# EREF — Standalone Volume Research Prompt

## Research Target

- **Company:** [COMPANY NAME]
- **Industry / industries:** [INDUSTRY OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Primary geographies:** [GEOGRAPHIES OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Research date:** [CURRENT DATE]
- **Special focus:** [OPTIONAL]

Conduct an **institutional-grade enterprise reverse-engineering study**.

This is not a conventional company profile, SWOT analysis, short equity-research note, or generic strategy exercise.

The objective is to take the enterprise apart component by component until its underlying machinery is understood.

## Non-Negotiable Depth Rule

Depth follows complexity and importance, not an arbitrary page or token budget.

If a subject requires 3 pages, write 3 pages. If it requires 20 pages, write 20 pages. If it requires 50 pages, write 50 pages.

Do not shorten later sections because earlier sections became long. A section ends only when the subject is adequately understood.

> **Completeness before concision.**

If output limits prevent completion, stop at a logical boundary and continue later without compressing the remaining work.

## Evidence Labels

Use:

- **CONFIRMED FACT**
- **COMPANY CLAIM**
- **THIRD-PARTY ESTIMATE**
- **ANALYTICAL INFERENCE**
- **HYPOTHESIS**
- **UNKNOWN**

Never present inference as fact.

## Source Priority

Prioritize annual reports, regulatory/securities filings, investor presentations, earnings materials, official corporate documents, public contracts, official product/service documentation, regulators, registries, court records, patent/trademark databases, procurement records, official technical sources, partner/customer/supplier sources, academic/industry research, reputable journalism, and specialist publications.

Triangulate important findings.

## Universal Questions

For every material component ask:

- **What?**
- **How?**
- **Who?**
- **Where?**
- **When?**
- **Why?**

Then analyze:

- **Structure**
- **Flow**
- **Control**
- **Economics**
- **Risk**

## Mandatory Trace Rules

### Follow the money
Identify whose money it is, which entity controls it, where it resides, when ownership/economic entitlement changes, when revenue is recognized, what costs are deducted, and who bears risk.

### Follow the physical flow
Trace materials, inventory, production, warehousing, logistics, delivery, returns, and service where relevant.

### Follow the data
Trace generation, storage, processing, access, analytics, governance, privacy, and decision use.

### Follow the legal entity
Identify which entity owns assets, signs contracts, employs people, holds licenses, earns revenue, bears liabilities, owns IP, and pays taxes.

### Follow the authority
Identify who can make, approve, block, override, or terminate important decisions.

### Follow the revenue
Customer need → commercial event → pricing → gross transaction/billing → direct costs → recognized revenue → contribution → operating expenses → profit → cash.

## Industry Adaptation

Adapt the framework to the company. Do not force fintech, software, manufacturing, banking, or other sector concepts where they do not apply.

## Anti-Speculation Rule

If evidence is unavailable, state what is known, label inference, preserve uncertainty, and identify evidence needed to resolve it.

# Volume III — Product, Service & Value-Flow Anatomy

**Execute Volume III only. Do not begin Volume IV.**

Primary question:

> **What exactly does the enterprise produce or provide, and how is value delivered from inputs to customer outcome?**

## III.1 Product / Service Universe
Decompose all material products, services, subscriptions, platforms, brands, business lines, hardware, software, APIs, consumables, aftermarket, financing, and ancillary offerings.

For each: customer, job, price, revenue, direct cost, margin, geography, regulation, dependencies, substitutes, and strategic purpose.

## III.2 Product Hierarchy
Map core products, supporting products, bundles, platforms, add-ons, consumables, service layers, premium tiers, and ecosystem products.

## III.3 Product Lifecycle
Trace ideation, development, launch, growth, maturity, maintenance, and discontinuation.

## III.4 Customer Journey
Trace Awareness → onboarding → purchase/contract → usage/delivery → support → repeat → cross-sell → renewal → exit.

## III.5 Core Value-Flow Reconstruction
Select representative high-value workflows.

Trace separately:
- customer-facing event,
- physical/material flow,
- money flow,
- data/information flow,
- internal operational state,
- accounting recognition,
- legal/regulatory responsibility.

## III.6 Transaction / Order / Service Teardown
For each step identify customer, entity, channel, supplier/input, facility/system, inventory, process, quality/control, pricing, direct cost, revenue recognition, delivery, settlement/payment, support, failure, timing, and risk.

## III.7 Product Dependencies
Map prerequisites, cross-sell, retention products, loss leaders, high-margin layers, ecosystem dependencies, and optionality.

## III.8 Product-Level Economics
Estimate revenue, gross margin, contribution, service burden, acquisition burden, retention, capital intensity, working capital, and failure/warranty/credit/fraud costs.

## III.9 Capacity / Supply Side
Where relevant, analyze scarce capacity such as factories, aircraft, rooms, GPU compute, skilled labor, drivers, sellers, beds, spectrum, or licensed professionals.

## III.10 Quality and Customer Outcome
Determine what constitutes success/failure for the customer and how quality is measured.

## III.11 Failure / Exception Paths
Analyze stockouts, defects, rejected applications, service outages, failed payments, refunds, warranties, recalls, disputes, fraud, and regulatory holds.

## III.12 Product-Market Evolution
Analyze original wedge, adjacent products, discontinued products, bundling, vertical integration, platformization, premium/down-market moves, and customer shifts.

## Final Questions
- What is the true core product?
- Which product acquires customers?
- Which product retains them?
- Which has the best economics?
- Which is strategically important despite being small?
- What does the customer actually buy: product, access, convenience, trust, status, network, financing, infrastructure, or something else?

**Completion test:** A reader should understand exactly what the enterprise delivers and how value moves from inputs to customer outcome.
