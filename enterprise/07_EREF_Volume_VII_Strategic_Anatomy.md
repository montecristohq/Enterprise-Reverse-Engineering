# EREF — Standalone Volume Research Prompt

## Research Target

- **Company:** [COMPANY NAME]
- **Industry / industries:** [INDUSTRY OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Primary geographies:** [GEOGRAPHIES OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Research date:** [CURRENT DATE]
- **Special focus:** [OPTIONAL]

Conduct an **institutional-grade enterprise reverse-engineering study**.

This is not a conventional company profile, SWOT analysis, short equity-research note, or generic strategy exercise.

The objective is to take the enterprise apart component by component until its underlying machinery is understood.

## Non-Negotiable Depth Rule

Depth follows complexity and importance, not an arbitrary page or token budget.

If a subject requires 3 pages, write 3 pages. If it requires 20 pages, write 20 pages. If it requires 50 pages, write 50 pages.

Do not shorten later sections because earlier sections became long. A section ends only when the subject is adequately understood.

> **Completeness before concision.**

If output limits prevent completion, stop at a logical boundary and continue later without compressing the remaining work.

## Evidence Labels

Use:

- **CONFIRMED FACT**
- **COMPANY CLAIM**
- **THIRD-PARTY ESTIMATE**
- **ANALYTICAL INFERENCE**
- **HYPOTHESIS**
- **UNKNOWN**

Never present inference as fact.

## Source Priority

Prioritize annual reports, regulatory/securities filings, investor presentations, earnings materials, official corporate documents, public contracts, official product/service documentation, regulators, registries, court records, patent/trademark databases, procurement records, official technical sources, partner/customer/supplier sources, academic/industry research, reputable journalism, and specialist publications.

Triangulate important findings.

## Universal Questions

For every material component ask:

- **What?**
- **How?**
- **Who?**
- **Where?**
- **When?**
- **Why?**

Then analyze:

- **Structure**
- **Flow**
- **Control**
- **Economics**
- **Risk**

## Mandatory Trace Rules

### Follow the money
Identify whose money it is, which entity controls it, where it resides, when ownership/economic entitlement changes, when revenue is recognized, what costs are deducted, and who bears risk.

### Follow the physical flow
Trace materials, inventory, production, warehousing, logistics, delivery, returns, and service where relevant.

### Follow the data
Trace generation, storage, processing, access, analytics, governance, privacy, and decision use.

### Follow the legal entity
Identify which entity owns assets, signs contracts, employs people, holds licenses, earns revenue, bears liabilities, owns IP, and pays taxes.

### Follow the authority
Identify who can make, approve, block, override, or terminate important decisions.

### Follow the revenue
Customer need → commercial event → pricing → gross transaction/billing → direct costs → recognized revenue → contribution → operating expenses → profit → cash.

## Industry Adaptation

Adapt the framework to the company. Do not force fintech, software, manufacturing, banking, or other sector concepts where they do not apply.

## Anti-Speculation Rule

If evidence is unavailable, state what is known, label inference, preserve uncertainty, and identify evidence needed to resolve it.

# Volume VII — Management, Culture, Competition, Moat, Risk & Strategic Evolution

**Execute Volume VII only. Do not begin Enterprise Reconstruction unless explicitly requested.**

Primary question:

> **Why does this enterprise win, what could destroy it, and what is it becoming?**

## VII.1 Founders / Historical Architects
Study backgrounds, motivations, operating philosophy, capital allocation, risk tolerance, long-term orientation, ownership, and governance influence.

## VII.2 Current Management
Analyze roles, tenure, experience, responsibilities, compensation, incentives, ownership, succession, and strategic influence.

## VII.3 Management System
Analyze strategic planning, budgeting, forecasting, KPIs, resource allocation, hiring, promotion, performance management, investment approval, risk approval, M&A, and geographic expansion.

## VII.4 Culture
Separate:
- **declared culture**
- **revealed culture**

Assess customer orientation, quality, frugality, transparency, autonomy, accountability, bureaucracy, speed, risk tolerance, innovation, long-termism, and internal politics.

## VII.5 Incentives
Study founders, executives, business-unit leaders, sales, product, engineering, operations, finance, compliance/risk, frontline workers, partners, and shareholders.

Identify conflicts and gameable metrics.

## VII.6 Competitive Universe
Segment:
- direct competitors,
- adjacent competitors,
- substitutes,
- vertical entrants,
- new technologies,
- low-cost entrants,
- premium entrants,
- public/government alternatives where relevant.

## VII.7 Competitor Teardowns
Compare customer, product, price, quality, distribution, geography, technology, assets, supply chain, brand, regulation, acquisition, economics, capital, scale, switching costs, and moat.

## VII.8 Why the Company Wins
Decompose:
- cost,
- product,
- quality,
- brand,
- trust,
- distribution,
- switching costs,
- scale,
- network effects,
- IP,
- technology,
- data,
- regulation,
- supplier/customer relationships,
- physical scarcity,
- speed,
- service,
- capital access,
- culture.

Separate:
- structural advantage,
- management choice,
- temporary advantage.

## VII.9 Moat Analysis
Score 0–5:
- network effects,
- scale economies,
- brand,
- trust,
- switching costs,
- regulation,
- distribution,
- IP,
- data,
- installed base,
- supply relationships,
- customer relationships,
- physical scarcity,
- cost position,
- ecosystem,
- learning curves,
- operational expertise,
- capital access.

Explain mechanism, evidence, durability, replicability, and failure conditions.

## VII.10 Replication Test
Imagine the company disappears.

For every key capability estimate:
- difficulty,
- time,
- capital,
- regulatory burden,
- technical burden,
- operational burden,
- talent burden,
- path dependence.

Classify:
- easy to buy,
- buildable,
- hard to reproduce,
- path-dependent,
- effectively non-replicable at comparable time/cost.

## VII.11 PESTLE
Analyze only material political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental factors.

## VII.12 Strategic Flywheels
Identify positive compounding mechanisms and verify them with evidence.

## VII.13 Negative Flywheels
Identify adverse compounding mechanisms.

## VII.14 Strategic Bottleneck
Ask:

> If every other part improved 50%, what single constraint would most limit future value creation?

Identify current and next bottleneck.

## VII.15 Risk Register
Analyze strategic, competitive, operational, supply, quality, financial, credit, liquidity, rates, FX, commodity, regulatory, legal, tax, cyber, data, IP, environmental, political, geopolitical, reputation, key-person, governance, execution, and concentration risk.

Score probability, severity, detectability, mitigation, residual risk, and time horizon.

## VII.16 Stress Tests
Model:
- revenue shock,
- margin compression,
- price war,
- major supplier/customer loss,
- regulation,
- operating-asset failure,
- cyber incident,
- management transition,
- substitute technology,
- sector-specific shocks.

## VII.17 Obsolescence
Ask what technology, regulation, business model, customer behavior, lower-cost production, platform shift, AI, automation, or substitutes could eliminate the company's advantage.

## VII.18 Strategic Optionality
Evaluate adjacencies.

Classify:
- natural adjacency,
- plausible,
- stretch,
- strategic fantasy.

## VII.19 What Is the Company Becoming?
Develop 3–6 competing strategic hypotheses and rank probabilities.

## VII.20 Five-Year and Ten-Year Strategic Map
Build base, strong execution, margin compression, disruption, regulatory shock, and strategic transformation scenarios.

## VII.21 Market Misconceptions
Identify at least five:
- conventional view,
- underlying reality,
- evidence,
- why misconception persists,
- implication.

## VII.22 Management & Capital-Allocation Judgment
Evaluate major historical decisions as:
- value creating,
- strategically necessary,
- questionable,
- too early to judge.

- What is the strongest moat?
- What moat is overrated?
- What is hardest to replicate?
