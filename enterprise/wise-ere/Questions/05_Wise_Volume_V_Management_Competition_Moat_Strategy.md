# Wise Enterprise Reverse-Engineering Project

## Standalone Research Prompt

You are conducting an **institutional-grade, forensic reverse-engineering study of Wise plc / Wise Group**.

This is not a conventional company profile, SWOT analysis, pitch-deck summary, short equity-research note, or generic fintech overview.

Your objective is to **take Wise apart component by component until the underlying machinery is understood**, and then reconstruct the relevant system for this volume.

Treat Wise as though it were a complex combination of:

- financial institution,
- payments network,
- software platform,
- treasury operation,
- regulated corporate group,
- operating organization,
- and economic machine.

The standard is not:

> “I understand what Wise does.”

The standard is:

> **“I understand how the relevant parts of Wise work, why they are structured that way, how they interact, where value is created, where money/data/authority/risk move, what they depend on, and what can break.”**

# Non-Negotiable Depth Rule

**Depth must follow the subject.**

Do not allocate equal space to sections.

Do not compress important material merely to keep the report short.

Do not aim for a predetermined page count, word count, or token count.

Do not shorten later sections because earlier sections became long.

Do not stop because the answer “feels long.”

A section ends only when its analytical questions have been adequately answered.

If a topic requires 2 pages, write 2 pages.

If it requires 10 pages, write 10 pages.

If it requires 30 pages, write 30 pages.

If understanding one revenue stream, license, payment rail, legal entity, technology subsystem, or operating process requires a lengthy prerequisite explanation, provide it.

**Completeness before concision.**

There is no reward for brevity and a substantial penalty for superficiality.

Do not confuse repetition with depth. Every additional paragraph must add evidence, mechanism, decomposition, comparison, causality, or synthesis.

Before ending any major section, ask:

> **Am I stopping because the subject is adequately understood, or because I am trying to keep the report short?**

If the answer is the latter, continue.

# Analytical Model

For every material component, answer:

## What?
What entities, products, systems, processes, assets, licenses, contracts, teams, accounts, datasets, rails, controls, or revenue streams exist?

## How?
How does it actually work? Explain mechanisms, not merely outcomes.

## Who?
Who owns, operates, regulates, controls, approves, depends on, profits from, or bears risk for it?

## Where?
Where does it exist legally, physically, technologically, economically, organizationally, and jurisdictionally?

## When?
What timing matters: funding, authorization, clearing, settlement, reconciliation, accounting recognition, reporting, liquidity cycles, product sequence, or strategic chronology?

## Why?
Why is it designed this way? What design logic, historical path dependence, economic rationale, or regulatory constraint explains the structure?

Then apply five cross-sectional lenses:

### Structure
What are the constituent pieces?

### Flow
What moves through them?

Possible flows include:

- money,
- accounting entries,
- data,
- customers,
- authority,
- liquidity,
- risk,

- information.

### Control
Who has decision rights? Who can authorize, block, override, or terminate an action?

### Economics
Who pays? Who receives? What does it cost? Where is value created and captured?

### Risk

# Evidence Classification

Explicitly distinguish:

- **CONFIRMED FACT** — directly supported by reliable evidence.
- **COMPANY CLAIM** — stated by Wise but not necessarily independently verified.
- **THIRD-PARTY ESTIMATE** — externally reported or estimated.
- **ANALYTICAL INFERENCE** — reasonably inferred from multiple known facts.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — possible explanation requiring additional evidence.
- **UNKNOWN** — available evidence is insufficient.

Never silently convert an inference into a fact.

When reputable sources disagree:

1. identify the disagreement,
2. compare source dates,
3. determine whether the difference reflects a change over time,
4. identify which source appears more authoritative,
5. preserve uncertainty where necessary.

Do not manufacture false precision.

# Causal-Chain Requirement

Do not merely state conclusions.

For every important conclusion, explain the causal mechanism.

Bad:

> “Wise benefits from economies of scale.”

Better:

1. Identify which costs scale.
2. Identify which remain variable.
3. Determine whether payment-rail costs decline with volume.
4. Determine whether internal matching/netting improves.
5. Determine whether liquidity efficiency improves.
6. Determine whether support/compliance expense scales linearly or sublinearly.
7. Examine historical evidence.
8. Identify possible diseconomies.
9. Connect the mechanism to unit economics.
10. Decide whether the effect is a durable advantage.

Apply this level of reasoning throughout.
