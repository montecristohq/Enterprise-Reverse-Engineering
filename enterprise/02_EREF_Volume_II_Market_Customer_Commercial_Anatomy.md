# EREF — Standalone Volume Research Prompt

## Research Target

- **Company:** [COMPANY NAME]
- **Industry / industries:** [INDUSTRY OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Primary geographies:** [GEOGRAPHIES OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Research date:** [CURRENT DATE]
- **Special focus:** [OPTIONAL]

Conduct an **institutional-grade enterprise reverse-engineering study**.

This is not a conventional company profile, SWOT analysis, short equity-research note, or generic strategy exercise.

The objective is to take the enterprise apart component by component until its underlying machinery is understood.

## Non-Negotiable Depth Rule

Depth follows complexity and importance, not an arbitrary page or token budget.

If a subject requires 3 pages, write 3 pages. If it requires 20 pages, write 20 pages. If it requires 50 pages, write 50 pages.

Do not shorten later sections because earlier sections became long. A section ends only when the subject is adequately understood.

> **Completeness before concision.**

If output limits prevent completion, stop at a logical boundary and continue later without compressing the remaining work.

## Evidence Labels

Use:

- **CONFIRMED FACT**
- **COMPANY CLAIM**
- **THIRD-PARTY ESTIMATE**
- **ANALYTICAL INFERENCE**
- **HYPOTHESIS**
- **UNKNOWN**

Never present inference as fact.

## Source Priority

Prioritize annual reports, regulatory/securities filings, investor presentations, earnings materials, official corporate documents, public contracts, official product/service documentation, regulators, registries, court records, patent/trademark databases, procurement records, official technical sources, partner/customer/supplier sources, academic/industry research, reputable journalism, and specialist publications.

Triangulate important findings.

## Universal Questions

For every material component ask:

- **What?**
- **How?**
- **Who?**
- **Where?**
- **When?**
- **Why?**

Then analyze:

- **Structure**
- **Flow**
- **Control**
- **Economics**
- **Risk**

## Mandatory Trace Rules

### Follow the money
Identify whose money it is, which entity controls it, where it resides, when ownership/economic entitlement changes, when revenue is recognized, what costs are deducted, and who bears risk.

### Follow the physical flow
Trace materials, inventory, production, warehousing, logistics, delivery, returns, and service where relevant.

### Follow the data
Trace generation, storage, processing, access, analytics, governance, privacy, and decision use.

### Follow the legal entity
Identify which entity owns assets, signs contracts, employs people, holds licenses, earns revenue, bears liabilities, owns IP, and pays taxes.

### Follow the authority
Identify who can make, approve, block, override, or terminate important decisions.

### Follow the revenue
Customer need → commercial event → pricing → gross transaction/billing → direct costs → recognized revenue → contribution → operating expenses → profit → cash.

## Industry Adaptation

Adapt the framework to the company. Do not force fintech, software, manufacturing, banking, or other sector concepts where they do not apply.

## Anti-Speculation Rule

If evidence is unavailable, state what is known, label inference, preserve uncertainty, and identify evidence needed to resolve it.

# Volume II — Market, Customer & Commercial Anatomy

**Execute Volume II only. Do not begin Volume III.**

Primary question:

> **Who pays this company, why do they pay it, how attractive is the market, and how does the enterprise acquire and retain demand?**

## II.1 Market Definition
Define the actual economic markets in which the company competes. Distinguish direct, adjacent, substitute, geographic, and customer-specific markets.

## II.2 Market Size and Structure
Analyze market revenue/volume, TAM/SAM/SOM where meaningful, growth, concentration, fragmentation, cyclicality, regulation, and geographic differences.

## II.3 Industry Value Pool
Identify where industry profits accumulate: manufacturers, distributors, platforms, service providers, intermediaries, infrastructure, aftermarkets.

## II.4 Customer Segmentation
Segment by B2C/B2B/B2G, geography, income/company size, industry, use case, sophistication, channel, value, and profitability.

For each: needs, behavior, frequency, spend/volume, price sensitivity, service burden, retention.

## II.5 Jobs to Be Done
For each segment identify triggering event, functional job, economic job, emotional/trust job where material, current workaround, switching trigger, and desired outcome.

## II.6 Buying Process
Map buyer, user, influencer, approver, procurement, budget holder, sales cycle, implementation cycle, and renewal process.

## II.7 Customer Acquisition
Analyze organic, paid, sales-led, partner-led, retail, channel, referral, brand, marketplace, and government procurement.

Determine CAC or useful proxy by channel where possible.

## II.8 Sales Architecture
Analyze self-service, SMB, mid-market, enterprise, field sales, channel sales, distributors, account management, solution engineering, and partner sales.

## II.9 Marketing
Analyze brand, performance marketing, content, events, sponsorship, referrals, community, and PR. Separate acquisition from brand maintenance.

## II.10 Distribution
Map stores, e-commerce, distributors, dealers, wholesalers, marketplaces, agents, brokers, banks, partners, app stores, and integrators.

For each: economics, customer ownership, data ownership, bargaining power, and scalability.

## II.11 Pricing
Analyze list vs realized price, discounts, rebates, bundles, subscriptions, usage pricing, dynamic pricing, enterprise contracts, financing, and cross-subsidies.

## II.12 Willingness to Pay and Price Elasticity
Determine why customers tolerate price, where they resist, and how price compares with alternatives.

## II.13 Retention and Churn
Analyze repeat behavior, contracts, switching costs, habits, integration, loyalty, service, product quality, and churn causes.

## II.14 Customer Concentration
Analyze top customers, revenue concentration, contract duration, bargaining power, and renewal risk.

## II.15 Competitive Market Structure
Apply Porter Five Forces to rivalry, buyers, suppliers, substitutes, and entrants. Keep deeper company-level competitor strategy for Volume VII.

## II.16 Commercial Flywheels
Identify whether acquisition, distribution density, installed base, brand, marketplace liquidity, or referral loops reinforce growth.

## Final Questions
- Who is the economically most important customer?
- What customer problem matters most?
- What is the strongest acquisition channel?
- Where does the company have genuine pricing power?
- What causes customers to stay?
- What market assumption is most critical to growth?

**Completion test:** A reader should understand demand: market, customer, buying behavior, pricing, acquisition, distribution, retention, and commercial power.
