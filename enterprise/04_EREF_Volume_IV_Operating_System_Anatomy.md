# EREF — Standalone Volume Research Prompt

## Research Target

- **Company:** [COMPANY NAME]
- **Industry / industries:** [INDUSTRY OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Primary geographies:** [GEOGRAPHIES OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Research date:** [CURRENT DATE]
- **Special focus:** [OPTIONAL]

Conduct an **institutional-grade enterprise reverse-engineering study**.

This is not a conventional company profile, SWOT analysis, short equity-research note, or generic strategy exercise.

The objective is to take the enterprise apart component by component until its underlying machinery is understood.

## Non-Negotiable Depth Rule

Depth follows complexity and importance, not an arbitrary page or token budget.

If a subject requires 3 pages, write 3 pages. If it requires 20 pages, write 20 pages. If it requires 50 pages, write 50 pages.

Do not shorten later sections because earlier sections became long. A section ends only when the subject is adequately understood.

> **Completeness before concision.**

If output limits prevent completion, stop at a logical boundary and continue later without compressing the remaining work.

## Evidence Labels

Use:

- **CONFIRMED FACT**
- **COMPANY CLAIM**
- **THIRD-PARTY ESTIMATE**
- **ANALYTICAL INFERENCE**
- **HYPOTHESIS**
- **UNKNOWN**

Never present inference as fact.

## Source Priority

Prioritize annual reports, regulatory/securities filings, investor presentations, earnings materials, official corporate documents, public contracts, official product/service documentation, regulators, registries, court records, patent/trademark databases, procurement records, official technical sources, partner/customer/supplier sources, academic/industry research, reputable journalism, and specialist publications.

Triangulate important findings.

## Universal Questions

For every material component ask:

- **What?**
- **How?**
- **Who?**
- **Where?**
- **When?**
- **Why?**

Then analyze:

- **Structure**
- **Flow**
- **Control**
- **Economics**
- **Risk**

## Mandatory Trace Rules

### Follow the money
Identify whose money it is, which entity controls it, where it resides, when ownership/economic entitlement changes, when revenue is recognized, what costs are deducted, and who bears risk.

### Follow the physical flow
Trace materials, inventory, production, warehousing, logistics, delivery, returns, and service where relevant.

### Follow the data
Trace generation, storage, processing, access, analytics, governance, privacy, and decision use.

### Follow the legal entity
Identify which entity owns assets, signs contracts, employs people, holds licenses, earns revenue, bears liabilities, owns IP, and pays taxes.

### Follow the authority
Identify who can make, approve, block, override, or terminate important decisions.

### Follow the revenue
Customer need → commercial event → pricing → gross transaction/billing → direct costs → recognized revenue → contribution → operating expenses → profit → cash.

## Industry Adaptation

Adapt the framework to the company. Do not force fintech, software, manufacturing, banking, or other sector concepts where they do not apply.

## Anti-Speculation Rule

If evidence is unavailable, state what is known, label inference, preserve uncertainty, and identify evidence needed to resolve it.

# Volume IV — Operating-System Anatomy

**Execute Volume IV only. Do not begin Volume V.**

Primary question:

> **How does the enterprise convert resources into delivered outcomes at scale?**

## IV.1 Operating Model
Map procurement, manufacturing, operations, logistics, quality, service, support, treasury, compliance, finance, legal, sales operations, field operations, R&D operations, vendor management, and internal services.

For each: mandate, inputs, processes, outputs, people, KPIs, cost, dependencies, bottlenecks, automation, outsourcing.

## IV.2 Core Process Maps
Identify and trace order-to-cash, procure-to-pay, concept-to-product, lead-to-sale, manufacture-to-delivery, application-to-service, transaction-to-settlement, and other industry-specific flows.

## IV.3 Supply Chain
Analyze raw materials, components, suppliers, sourcing geography, contracts, qualification, lead times, logistics, concentration, dual sourcing, and geopolitical exposure.

## IV.4 Procurement
Analyze buying power, sourcing strategy, commodity exposure, contracts, supplier economics, and procurement organization.

## IV.5 Manufacturing / Service Delivery
Where relevant: plants, facilities, capacity, utilization, throughput, yield, downtime, maintenance, automation, labor, energy, safety, and quality. For service companies, use the analogous service-capacity model.

## IV.6 Logistics & Fulfillment
Analyze inbound logistics, warehousing, inventory placement, outbound logistics, last-mile, delivery SLAs, returns, and reverse logistics.

## IV.7 Inventory
Analyze raw materials, WIP, finished goods, safety stock, turns, obsolescence, shrinkage, and seasonality.

## IV.8 Quality
Analyze quality gates, defect rates, recalls, warranty, testing, certification, complaints, and continuous improvement.

## IV.9 Customer Support / Field Service
Analyze channels, staffing, self-service, escalation, repair, maintenance, warranties, and service economics.

## IV.10 Outsourcing
Determine what is outsourced and why: manufacturing, logistics, support, professional services, contractors, BPO, and facilities.

## IV.11 Facilities and Physical Footprint
Map offices, factories, warehouses, labs, branches, stores, depots, service centers, and leased/owned assets.

## IV.12 Workforce Operations
Analyze headcount by function/geography, labor intensity, shifts, productivity, contractors, unions/works councils, critical skills, and turnover.

## IV.13 Process Automation
Assess robotics, workflow automation, AI, scheduling, optimization, self-service, and straight-through processing.

## IV.14 Operating Leverage
Classify processes as nearly automatic, sublinear, linear, step-fixed, or disproportionate.

## IV.15 Bottlenecks
Use Theory of Constraints:

> If every other operational component improved by 50%, what single constraint would still cap output or growth?

Identify current and next bottleneck.

## IV.16 Resilience
Stress supplier failure, facility outage, labor disruption, logistics breakdown, demand spike, demand collapse, utility failure, recall, and regulatory shutdown.

## Final Questions
- What is the operating core?
- What physical/process constraint matters most?
- Where does operating leverage actually come from?
- What is hardest to scale?
- What is hardest to replicate?
- Which external dependency could stop operations fastest?

**Completion test:** A reader should understand how resources become delivered outcomes, at what cost, through which bottlenecks, with what resilience.
