# EREF — Standalone Volume Research Prompt

## Research Target

- **Company:** [COMPANY NAME]
- **Industry / industries:** [INDUSTRY OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Primary geographies:** [GEOGRAPHIES OR LEAVE FOR AGENT TO DETERMINE]
- **Research date:** [CURRENT DATE]
- **Special focus:** [OPTIONAL]

Conduct an **institutional-grade enterprise reverse-engineering study**.

This is not a conventional company profile, SWOT analysis, short equity-research note, or generic strategy exercise.

The objective is to take the enterprise apart component by component until its underlying machinery is understood.

## Non-Negotiable Depth Rule

Depth follows complexity and importance, not an arbitrary page or token budget.

If a subject requires 3 pages, write 3 pages. If it requires 20 pages, write 20 pages. If it requires 50 pages, write 50 pages.

Do not shorten later sections because earlier sections became long. A section ends only when the subject is adequately understood.

> **Completeness before concision.**

If output limits prevent completion, stop at a logical boundary and continue later without compressing the remaining work.

## Evidence Labels

Use:

- **CONFIRMED FACT**
- **COMPANY CLAIM**
- **THIRD-PARTY ESTIMATE**
- **ANALYTICAL INFERENCE**
- **HYPOTHESIS**
- **UNKNOWN**

Never present inference as fact.

## Source Priority

Prioritize annual reports, regulatory/securities filings, investor presentations, earnings materials, official corporate documents, public contracts, official product/service documentation, regulators, registries, court records, patent/trademark databases, procurement records, official technical sources, partner/customer/supplier sources, academic/industry research, reputable journalism, and specialist publications.

Triangulate important findings.

## Universal Questions

For every material component ask:

- **What?**
- **How?**
- **Who?**
- **Where?**
- **When?**
- **Why?**

Then analyze:

- **Structure**
- **Flow**
- **Control**
- **Economics**
- **Risk**

## Mandatory Trace Rules

### Follow the money
Identify whose money it is, which entity controls it, where it resides, when ownership/economic entitlement changes, when revenue is recognized, what costs are deducted, and who bears risk.

### Follow the physical flow
Trace materials, inventory, production, warehousing, logistics, delivery, returns, and service where relevant.

### Follow the data
Trace generation, storage, processing, access, analytics, governance, privacy, and decision use.

### Follow the legal entity
Identify which entity owns assets, signs contracts, employs people, holds licenses, earns revenue, bears liabilities, owns IP, and pays taxes.

### Follow the authority
Identify who can make, approve, block, override, or terminate important decisions.

### Follow the revenue
Customer need → commercial event → pricing → gross transaction/billing → direct costs → recognized revenue → contribution → operating expenses → profit → cash.

## Industry Adaptation

Adapt the framework to the company. Do not force fintech, software, manufacturing, banking, or other sector concepts where they do not apply.

## Anti-Speculation Rule

If evidence is unavailable, state what is known, label inference, preserve uncertainty, and identify evidence needed to resolve it.

# Volume VI — Economic & Capital Anatomy

**Execute Volume VI only. Do not begin Volume VII.**

Primary question:

> **How does the enterprise convert activity into revenue, profit, cash flow, and return on capital?**

Give financially important topics unlimited depth relative to evidence.

## VI.1 Multi-Year Financial History
Prefer 5–10 years where useful: revenue, segments, gross profit, Opex, EBITDA, EBIT, pretax income, tax, net income, EPS, operating cash flow, CapEx, FCF, cash, debt, shares, dividends/buybacks, and industry-specific operating metrics.

## VI.2 Revenue Taxonomy
For each stream: payer, segment, product, trigger, pricing, contract, geography, volume driver, recognition, direct costs, margin, recurrence, cyclicality, concentration, sustainability.

## VI.3 Revenue Flow
Trace representative commercial activity into accounting revenue and cash.

## VI.4 Pricing Economics
Analyze price vs volume, realized pricing, discounting, mix, inflation pass-through, elasticity, and cross-subsidies.

## VI.5 Cost Architecture
Break into direct/product, labor, supply, logistics, technology, service, risk/loss, sales/marketing, R&D, and corporate costs. Classify variable, fixed, semi-variable, and step-fixed.

## VI.6 Unit Economics
Identify the correct unit: customer, subscriber, order, store, route, loan, account, product, project, factory, tonne, MW, or industry-specific equivalent.

Calculate revenue/unit, margin/unit, CAC, servicing cost, LTV, utilization, yield, payback, and incremental margin where possible.

## VI.7 Segment Economics
Analyze economics by product, geography, customer, channel, facility, maturity, and project type.

## VI.8 Income Statement Teardown
Analyze every material line and its operational cause.

## VI.9 Balance Sheet Teardown
Analyze cash, receivables, inventory, loans, investments, PP&E, intangibles, goodwill, payables, deferred revenue, provisions, debt, leases, pensions, customer liabilities, regulatory capital, and equity.

## VI.10 Working Capital
Analyze receivable days, payable days, inventory days, deferred revenue, advances, progress payments, and seasonality.

## VI.11 Capital Intensity
Separate maintenance CapEx, growth CapEx, working capital, regulatory capital, facilities, fleets, stores, data centers, inventory, and R&D.

## VI.12 Cash Flow
Reconcile accounting profit to shareholder free cash flow.

## VI.13 Debt & Funding
Analyze maturities, rates, covenants, revolvers, leases, securitizations, deposits, project finance, supplier finance, and customer advances.

## VI.14 Capital Allocation
Analyze organic investment, R&D, CapEx, M&A, divestitures, debt repayment, dividends, buybacks, cash accumulation, and venture investments.

## VI.15 Return on Capital
Calculate ROIC, ROE, ROA, incremental ROIC, segment returns, and acquisition returns where meaningful.

## VI.16 One Currency Unit of Revenue
Show where $1/€1/£1 of revenue goes.

## VI.17 Economic Driver Tree
Build the 5–10 variables that explain most financial outcomes.

## VI.18 Scenario Model
Model strong growth, slow growth, price competition, cost inflation, recession/demand shock, operating leverage, and sector-specific shocks.

## VI.19 Sensitivity Analysis
Test the most important drivers.

## VI.20 Revenue Quality
Score predictability, recurrence, retention, pricing power, concentration, cyclicality, margin, capital intensity, regulatory risk, and disruption risk.

## VI.21 Normalized Profitability
Separate structural economics from temporary macro and accounting effects.

## VI.22 Valuation-Relevant Economics
Without forcing a buy/sell view, analyze growth durability, margin ceiling, reinvestment, capital intensity, FCF quality, cyclicality, dilution, optionality, and long-term returns.

## Final Questions
- What is the real economic engine?
- Which revenue stream is highest quality?
- Where does operating leverage come from?
- Is growth capital-light or capital-intensive?
