Conventions governing the whole document

The evidence ladder

Every material claim carries one of eight labels. They are not decoration — they are load-bearing, and no inference is silently promoted to a confirmation. If a volume says CONFIRMED IMPLEMENTATION, a reader must be able to go and look.

LabelMeaning
CONFIRMED IMPLEMENTATIONVerified in source, schema or specification. The named file, function, table or clause is identifiable
MEASURED RESULTA benchmark or measurement with its methodology and conditions stated
DOCUMENTED BEHAVIORStated in official documentation but not verified in source
SOURCE-CODE INFERENCEReasoned from reading code, with the reasoning shown
ANALYTICAL INFERENCEReasoned from multiple known facts, with the reasoning shown
MAINTAINER CLAIMAsserted by the project, vendor or maintainer. Not evidence of implementation
HYPOTHESISPlausible, requiring further evidence
UNKNOWNThe evidence is insufficient — flagged, never papered over

The labels are distinguished by mark before colour — a filled plate for what was verified, an outline for what was documented, a dotted rule for what was inferred, a knockout for what is not known. Two of the seventeen palettes would defeat a colour-only encoding: Newsprint sets both accents to the same grey, and Midnight inverts the ramps.

Version discipline

The version is pinned: ⟨version · commit⟩. Behaviour is never silently mixed across incompatible releases. Where a claim's behaviour changed across releases, the release boundary is stated and the side being described is named. Where this study spanned a version change mid-research, the change is logged in Appendix D rather than absorbed silently.

The evidence ceiling

Source openness — ⟨position⟩
⟨What the source-openness field permits. A study of an OPAQUE technology cannot reach CONFIRMED IMPLEMENTATION on anything, and must say so here rather than discovering it at Volume IV.⟩

UNKNOWN is a valid and frequently correct answer. A study of an opaque technology that reaches it forty times has done its job; one that reaches it never has not.

VI.17Transplant verdicts

Apply the environment question before each verdict: did this work because of the mechanism itself, or because of the team size, hardware, traffic profile, operational maturity or surrounding ecosystem? Strip the environment out, then judge.
